what_i_learned: |
  Today I spent more time working with the Padonia PM2.5 dataset in Google Colab and focused on creating and analyzing visualizations to better understand the data. I learned how to organize air quality measurements by month and create graphs that show how PM2.5 concentrations change throughout the year. By creating scatter plots and bar graphs, I was able to identify trends and compare pollution levels across different time periods. I also learned how data visualization can be used as an exploratory data analysis technique before building machine learning models. In addition, I continued practicing Python programming skills, including manipulating data frames, grouping data, and troubleshooting code when visualizations did not display correctly.
blockers: |
  One challenge I faced was deciding how to best represent the data visually so that the trends were easy to understand. At times, my code produced errors that prevented graphs from displaying correctly, which required me to carefully review variable names, data formatting, and plotting commands. Another challenge was interpreting the meaning behind the patterns shown in the graphs. While I could see differences between months, I had to think critically about what environmental factors might contribute to those changes and how those trends could affect future PM2.5 predictions.
reflection: |
 Today’s work helped me appreciate the importance of data visualization in both research and machine learning. Before building a predictive model, it is important to understand the data and identify patterns that may influence the results. Creating graphs allowed me to move beyond looking at rows of numbers and instead see meaningful trends in air quality over time. I also improved my confidence with Python and Google Colab by solving coding issues independently and experimenting with different ways to display the data. By the end of the day, I had a stronger understanding of the Padonia dataset and how visual analysis can help guide future modeling decisions in our PM2.5 prediction project..
