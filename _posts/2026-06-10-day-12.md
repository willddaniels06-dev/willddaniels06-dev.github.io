what_i_learned: |
  Today I learned more about the environmental science side of our project by exploring how satellite observations and meteorological factors can be used to predict PM2.5 concentrations. I studied Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) and learned that it measures the amount of aerosols in the atmosphere by observing how particles affect sunlight. I also learned about the Planetary Boundary Layer (PBL), which is the lowest part of the atmosphere where weather conditions and pollutants interact most directly with the Earth’s surface. Understanding these concepts helped me see why satellite and weather data are valuable inputs for AI-driven air quality prediction models. I also learned how these variables can influence PM2.5 levels and improve the accuracy of machine learning predictions.
Blockers: |
  The main challenge I faced today was understanding some of the scientific terminology and atmospheric concepts. Since I am still developing my background in environmental science, concepts such as AOD, PBL, and aerosol transport required extra reading and discussion to fully understand. It was also challenging to connect these scientific measurements to the data columns we use in our datasets and see how they contribute to PM2.5 predictions.
reflection: |
  Today’s work helped me realize how interdisciplinary our project is. It combines artificial intelligence, data science, satellite technology, meteorology, and environmental science. Learning about AOD and PBL gave me a deeper appreciation for how satellites can provide information about air quality over large areas, even where ground monitoring stations may be limited. Although the concepts were challenging at first, I feel that I now have a stronger understanding of the environmental factors that influence PM2.5 concentrations and why they are important for building accurate prediction models. This knowledge helped me connect the science behind the data with the machine learning techniques we have been studying throughout the program.
