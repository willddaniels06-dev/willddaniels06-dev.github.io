what_i_learned: |
  Today was the second-to-last day of our online SAIRI classes, and it gave me an opportunity to review and connect everything I have learned throughout the program. I revisited the complete workflow of our PM2.5 prediction project, from collecting and merging datasets to creating visualizations, performing feature engineering, splitting data into training and testing sets, and evaluating machine learning models using metrics such as R² and RMSE. I also reflected on how satellite data, meteorological variables, and air quality measurements work together to help predict PM2.5 concentrations. Throughout the day, I strengthened my understanding of how each step contributes to building an effective AI model and how researchers use data science to solve real-world environmental problems.
blockers: |
  One challenge I faced was bringing together all of the concepts we have learned over the past two weeks into one clear understanding of the project. We covered many topics, including Python programming, data cleaning, visualization, satellite observations, machine learning, and model evaluation. While I understood each topic individually, it took time to see how they all fit together in a single workflow. Another challenge was preparing to explain the project in a way that others can easily understand, especially when discussing technical concepts.

 As the second-to-last day of online classes, today made me realize how much progress I have made since the beginning of the program. Looking back on the program so far, I am proud of the skills I have developed and the challenges I have overcome. I feel more confident in my ability to work with data and excited to continue learning as we approach the final stages of the project.
