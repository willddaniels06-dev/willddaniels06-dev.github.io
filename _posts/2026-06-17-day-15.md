what_i_learned: |
  Today was the final day of the online portion of the SAIRI Institute, and it gave me the opportunity to reflect on everything I have learned throughout the program. Over the past several weeks, I developed skills in Python programming, data analysis, data visualization, and machine learning while working on our PM2.5 prediction project. I learned how to import, clean, merge, and analyze datasets in Google Colab, create visualizations to identify trends, and evaluate machine learning models using metrics such as R² and RMSE. I also gained knowledge about environmental science concepts including PM2.5, Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD), and the Planetary Boundary Layer (PBL), and how satellite and meteorological data can be used to improve air quality predictions. Today’s activities helped reinforce how all of these concepts work together within a real-world research project.

  One challenge today was reviewing the large amount of information we covered throughout the online portion of the institute and making sure I fully understood how each part contributed to the overall project. There were many technical concepts, coding skills, and scientific ideas that required continued practice and review. Another challenge was preparing to communicate what I learned in a clear and concise way, especially when explaining machine learning and environmental science concepts to people who may not be familiar with them.

 Completing the online portion of the SAIRI Institute feels like a major accomplishment. At the beginning of the program, I had limited experience with many of the tools and concepts we used, but I now feel much more confident working with datasets, coding in Python, creating visualizations, and understanding the basics of machine learning. Beyond the technical skills, I learned how AI can be applied to solve important environmental problems such as predicting PM2.5 air pollution. I also gained experience collaborating with mentors and peers, asking questions, and working through challenges when problems arose. Looking back, I am proud of the progress I have made and excited to continue building on these skills during the next phase of the program.
