what_i_learned: |
  Today was the first day of the SAIRI Institute, and I learned about the goals and structure of the program. We were introduced to the different research projects and how artificial intelligence can be used to solve real-world problems. I learned that my group’s project focuses on predicting PM2.5 air pollution levels using satellite and meteorological data. The mentors explained the importance of air quality research and how pollution can impact human health and the environment. I was introduced to the research process and learned that our project would involve data analysis, machine learning, and collaboration with teammates. I learned about the expectations for participation, professionalism, and teamwork throughout the program. We discussed the role of satellite observations in environmental monitoring and how they can provide useful information for air quality prediction. I also learned about some of the tools we would be using, including Google Colab and Python. Through orientation activities, I became familiar with the schedule and structure of the institute. By the end of the day, I had a much clearer understanding of what we would be working on during the summer and how the project would contribute to environmental research.
blockers: |
  Since this was the first day of the program, one of my biggest challenges was processing the large amount of new information that was presented. There were many unfamiliar terms related to artificial intelligence, machine learning, environmental science, and satellite data. It was sometimes difficult to understand how all of these topics connected to our project because everything was still very new. I also had to learn how the program was organized and what would be expected of me throughout the institute. Adjusting to a new group of peers and mentors took some time as I became comfortable participating in discussions. Some of the technical concepts were introduced quickly, which made it challenging to fully understand them right away. I had questions about how AI models are built and how they would be used in our project. Learning new platforms and tools also required some adjustment. Another challenge was understanding the overall timeline of the project and what milestones we would need to complete. Despite these challenges, I knew that asking questions and staying engaged would help me overcome them.

reflection: |
 My first day in the SAIRI Institute was both exciting and informative. Although there was a lot of information to absorb, I enjoyed learning about the different research opportunities and meeting the people I would be working with throughout the program. I was especially interested in the PM2.5 project because it combines artificial intelligence with environmental science to address a real-world issue. The orientation activities helped me feel more connected to the program and gave me a better understanding of its goals. I appreciated hearing from the mentors and learning about their experiences in research and technology. While some of the concepts seemed challenging at first, I felt motivated to learn more and develop new skills. I recognized that I would need to step outside my comfort zone and be willing to ask questions when I did not understand something. The first day showed me that this program would be a valuable opportunity to grow both academically and professionally. I am excited to learn more about AI, machine learning, and environmental data throughout the summer. Overall, I left the first day feeling motivated, curious, and ready to contribute to the project with my teammates and mentors.
