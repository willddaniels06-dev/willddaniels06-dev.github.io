what_i_learned: |
  Today I focused on evaluating the performance of our PM2.5 prediction model and learned more about the metrics used to determine how well a machine learning model performs. I explored R² (coefficient of determination) and RMSE (Root Mean Square Error) and learned that R² measures how much of the variation in PM2.5 concentrations is explained by the model, while RMSE measures the average prediction error. I also learned how to compare actual PM2.5 values with predicted values using scatter plots and how these visualizations help identify whether a model is making accurate predictions. In addition, I gained a better understanding of the importance of splitting data into training and testing sets so that model performance can be evaluated fairly.
blockers: |
  One of the biggest challenges I faced was interpreting what the evaluation metrics actually meant. While calculating R² and RMSE is straightforward using Python, understanding whether the values indicate strong or weak model performance required additional research and discussion. I also found it challenging to connect the numerical metrics with the graphs we created and understand how they both tell a story about model accuracy. Sometimes it was difficult to determine whether a model’s errors were acceptable or if improvements were needed.
reflection: |
  Today helped me understand that building a machine learning model is only part of the process; evaluating the model is equally important. A model may produce predictions, but without metrics like R² and RMSE, it is difficult to know whether those predictions can be trusted. Working through these concepts helped me think more critically about data analysis and model performance. I now have a stronger appreciation for how researchers test and validate AI models before using them to make predictions about real-world problems such as air pollution. This experience strengthened both my understanding of machine learning and my ability to explain technical results in a meaningful way.
